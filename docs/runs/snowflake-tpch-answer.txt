## Findings

**Revenue by nation (all order years, 1992–1998 partial):** Revenue from orders is essentially flat across all 25 TPC-H nations — every nation sits between $8.47B and $8.96B, a spread of only ~5.6% top to bottom. France leads narrowly ($8.96B), Saudi Arabia trails ($8.47B), but there's no meaningful concentration — this is a uniform ~4% share per nation (1/25), consistent with TPC-H's synthetic, uniformly-distributed data generator rather than any real economic signal (q1).

**Has the mix shifted over time?** No. Restricting to the five full order years (1993–1997, excluding the partial 1992 and 1998 boundary years) and computing each nation's share of total yearly revenue:

- Every nation's share stays within a **3.8%–4.2% band**, with standard deviation across years of at most **0.08 percentage points** (q4).
- Nominal "rank" (1st through 25th) does bounce around a lot year to year — e.g., Canada, Indonesia, and France each hit #1 in at least one year but also fall to rank 14–16 in another — but that volatility is just noise around a near-identical ~4% share, not a real shift in mix. When shares are this close together, small revenue differences flip ranks without reflecting any structural change.
- No nation shows a trend (steadily rising or falling share); the swings look like random variation, not a directional shift.

## Answer

No single nation or group of nations dominates order revenue — all 25 nations contribute roughly equal shares (~4% each), and that mix has **not meaningfully shifted** across the order years in the data. Year-over-year rank changes are just noise on top of an essentially uniform distribution. This flatness is a known property of TPC-H's synthetic data generator (customers/orders are distributed uniformly across nations), so it reflects the benchmark's design rather than any real business dynamic.
